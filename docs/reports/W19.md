# W19: names, a map, the hidden wood, and the grass fill

Written for the owner. Four things were asked for and all four are in. One of them turned into a real
performance fix, and one of them had to be moved across the map after the first attempt broke the castle.

## 1. Everything has a name

`data/places.json` names **nine quarters, twenty-six places and four river reaches**, read by
`Places` (`scripts/systems/places.gd`) and by the map. Nothing is named twice and nothing is named in two
places: a test checks that every name is used once and that every named place pointing at a site points at
a site that is really on the map, so a name cannot quietly outlive the thing it names.

North is **-Z**, which was not a decision made this week: `north_watch` has sat at z = -280 and `south_ring`
at z = +350 since week one, so the compass follows the data.

The quarters, clockwise from north: **The Drowned March** (the river country), **Morrowfield** (the
castle's own ground), **The Greenwatch** (the forest road), **Wyrdreach** (the hidden valley),
**The Still Meadow**, **Hollowmere Downs**, **The Ashen Flats** (the farms and the Overlook) and
**The Fallow Marches** (the empty north-west, which is where the hidden village went). The bowl in the
middle, where you wake, is **Mournhearth**.

New names worth knowing: **Thornhollow** (the hidden village), **Thornwood** (the wood round it),
**The Girdle** (the wall of rock round that), **Wyrm's Rest** (the dead dragon), **The Sunken Wheel**
(the fallen hoop of stone), **Riverend** (the village on the bank), **The Quiet Bell**, **The Cold Watch**,
**The Green Watch**, **The Still Watch**, **The Sunken Field**, **The Cutting**, **Ashfarm**.

## 2. The map

Top-left corner, always on, 320 m across. **M** opens the whole Vale; **M** or **Esc** closes it. North is
up and never rotates, the eight bearings are marked round the edge, and the arrow is you, pointing where
you are looking. The full map writes the quarter you are in and the nearest named place with its bearing
("Thornhollow, 58 m south-west").

**The part that matters: the map keeps no list of its own.** The ridge, the pond, the river and the flat
ground come out of `data/areas/vale.json`; every building dot comes out of `SiteRegistry`, coloured by the
folder its layout lives in; the names come out of `data/places.json`; and the wood is read off the live
`Forest` node. So anything added to the Vale is on the map the next time it loads, without anyone editing
the map. A test asserts exactly that, and a negative control that gave the map its own list was caught.

## 3. The hidden wood, and what it cost to place

**Thornwood** is thousands of trees in one place, 95 m across, round a 36 m clearing, with the density rising
toward the outer edge so that it is a wall from outside and a room from within. Inside the clearing stands
**The Girdle**, a wall of crag, boulder and standing stone 56 m across with one gap in it, a fire either
side of the gap. Inside that is **Thornhollow**: seven buildings, a pyre in the middle, five people who
would rather you had not come.

The forest is **6,157 trees** now, against 1,180 before — see section 6, and section 7 for why an
earlier draft of this report would have claimed 11,624.

Three things had to be solved and none of them was obvious:

- **The Vale had no room for it.** The south-east was the emptiest ground, so that is where it went first.
  Its plateau's skirt reached 47 m, Castle Morrow's flat top started 47 m away, and the suite caught the
  castle's own ground being tilted from 125 m off. The castle's skirt is 158 m long and there is no space
  beside it. The wood moved to the **Fallow Marches**, the only quarter with 53 m of clearance from every
  site, 133 m from any other plateau and 55 m from the river.
- **Separate rocks cannot make a wall.** Every pair of sites must keep 1.5 m between their real footprints,
  and a crag's footprint is 13 m across, so a ring tight enough to read as a wall is a ring of sites that
  overlap — the suite listed eleven such pairs. The Girdle is therefore **one layout** of twenty-one rock
  groups; pieces inside a layout may touch, which is what a rock wall does.
- **A wood you can walk through hides nothing.** Tree colliders went to the trees nearest the origin, and
  this wood is 290 m from the origin, so every collider would have been spent back at the spawn. `Forest`
  now takes `collider_anchors`; the origin is always one, the wood is the other, and the count went 60 to
  220.

It is a hamlet, not a town. The clearing inside the wall is 56 m across and a cottage's footprint is 12 m
of it, so **seven** is what fits. Wyrdhollow is six.

## 4. The two set pieces

- **Wyrm's Rest**, on Hollowmere's south-western outskirts: twenty metres of spine, ribs leaning off it, a
  wing bone folded under, a skull with its jaw open. Fenced off on all four sides by whoever found it, with
  a dead fire at the fence where they sat and looked at it, a shrine stone, and offerings. There is no
  dragon in the kit; this one is made of blocks, pillars and headstones in bone.
- **The Sunken Wheel**, alone in the eastern waste 163 m from the nearest road: a hoop of worked stone eight
  metres across that stood upright once, broken open on one side, its lower arc buried. There is an
  inscription at its foot and the inscription has worn off.

## 5. The grass fill — the W19 profiling job, done

The sprint probe was failing two assertions: cells within 50 m of a sprinting player were still empty, and
too many frames went over 4 ms. **I checked this against the last commit before touching anything, and it
failed there too** — 4 unfilled cells and 44 to 92 frames over 4 ms, against limits of 0 and 75. It was not
caused by the wood; with the wood switched off the numbers did not move.

Profiling said the cost was the **fixed cost of preparing a cell**, not the work inside it: `prepare` builds
a height grid, samples the zones and gathers the nearby trees, and the lattice was paying that 81 times.
Smaller cells were tried first and made it worse (10 and 11 unfilled at 9 m and 8 m). Bigger cells fixed it.

The lattice is now **15 m cells, not 12**, with every density cap and the density multiplier raised to match
the larger cell, so the field is not thinner for it:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| unfilled cells within 50 m, sprinting | 4 | **0** |
| working frames over 4 ms (of ~1500) | 44–92 | **0** |
| 95th-percentile fill frame | 3.8–4.2 ms | **2.7 ms** |
| frames doing fill work at all | 1500 | 904 |
| grass instances per m² | 2.30 | **2.34** |

Two smaller fixes in the same area, both real and both now needed: the per-cell tree gather was walking
**every** tree in the level (fine at 600, not at 6,157) and is now read out of 64 m buckets; and the wood's
own spacing check was walking every tree already planted, which is quadratic at five thousand candidates
and is now read out of 1.6 m buckets.

**What I traded:** the lattice's diagonal reach went from about 60 m to about 54 m. The shader's fade starts
at 50 m, so the fade still completes inside the lattice — but if you ever see grass stop short on a diagonal
while sprinting, that is the number to raise.

## 6. The other open quarters, and the trees you can lean on

The owner asked for the open quarters to be filled with forest, thousands of trees at a time, so woods are
**data** now (`data/forest/vale_groves.json`) rather than four exports each. Three more went in:

- **Greenwatch Holt** (east, 86 m). The Greenwatch was named for its wood and had no wood in it — a tower,
  a road and four camps on open ground. This is the wood the name was promising.
- **Mirewood** (north, 78 m), between the cairns and the river bank. You see the river through the trunks a
  long time before you reach it.
- **The Long Hedge** (west, 64 m), under the Overlook. Thickens a quarter the scatter already covered into
  something you walk round rather than through.

The forest is **6,157 trees**, from 621 at the start of the week.

**And the trees are solid now.** They were not: a body was made per tree at load for the few hundred
nearest a fixed point and never moved, so which trees you could lean on was decided before the game
started. That was fine when the forest was six hundred trees round the spawn and useless across five woods,
three of them 300 m away. The bodies are a **pool of 320** that follows you within 30 m, re-dealt every 8 m
you walk (D-038). The probe prints `dealt` and `nearest_solid` every run so this cannot quietly stop
working, and a control that made the pool deal nothing was caught.

## 7. The bug that cost the most, and looked like nothing

`(dict[key] as PackedVector2Array).append(p)` appends to a **copy** and throws it away. `forest.gd` has
carried a comment warning about exactly this for weeks. I walked into it three times in one night — the
wood's spacing check, the tree index behind the collider pool, and the grass cluster index — and **all
three looked like they worked**:

- The woods placed **every one of 15,000 candidates**, stacked on top of each other, because every spacing
  check read an empty bucket. The tree count looked generous. It was 15,000 trees in the space for 5,000.
- The collider pool could not find a tree anywhere in the Vale, which is how I found the rest of it.
- The grass clustering had quietly stopped happening.

Every bucket now holds a plain `Array`, and the spacing rule has a test that measures the closest pair of
trees in a crowded grove. **The real lesson is not about GDScript** (D-039): all three were optimisations,
and an optimisation that silently does nothing looks exactly like one that works. The spacing index had no
test. The two things that *did* have tests — the grass fill and the grove's tree count — both reported
**better** numbers with the bug than without it.

## Verification

- **Suite 446 → 474, 0 failures**, 0 script errors in either stream, per-file sums match (57 files).
- **21 negative controls**, one per new claim — south-up map, a closed ring, a hundred trees instead of
  thousands, colliders ignoring their anchors, a name pointing at nothing, two places sharing a name, the
  village losing its flat floor, the dragon losing its fence, and seven more. **All 21 were caught.** The
  first attempt at the fence control replaced one of the two fences and was not caught; that was a bad
  control, not a bad test, and redoing it properly caught it.
- Build exported and verified: `PROBE` prints all **7** lines, 0 errors.

## Still not done

- **Nobody has looked at any of this.** The map, the wood, the Girdle, the dragon and the wheel have only
  ever been drawn by a null renderer. A thirty-second clip of walking into Thornwood from the south-east
  would settle more than the whole of this report.
- **The minimap's look is untested.** It is drawn in code with a plain frame; whether 168 px is the right
  size and whether the dots read at that scale is a thing to see, not to assert.
- **Load time is 7.4 seconds.** The valley is silent while it builds, and five woods did not help. It was
  already 5.1 s before tonight and it is the oldest unaddressed cost in the project.
- **Mountains** still do not exist, and the Vale still has no room for them (D-032, W16).
- The outstanding list is otherwise unchanged: O-2 (a Freesound key), O-3 (story sign-off), O-4 (an icon).
